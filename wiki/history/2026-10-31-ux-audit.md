# 2026-10-31: UX audit by five auditor agents

## What was done

The user asked for a team of user-experience advocates to load, manipulate and review the app
and produce a roadmap. Five agents ran in parallel, one lens each, read-only: GET requests to
production, and headless Chrome against a Vite proxy of production with every Stash-writing
route blocked and no app-database action clicked ([[prod-checks-read-only]]). Their full
reports (about 140 KB, with tables and file:line evidence) are kept as
`docs/audits/2026-10-31-ux/{server,client,phone,desktop,ia}.md`; this page is the record and
[[roadmap]] holds the plan.

| Lens | Measured / walked |
|---|---|
| Server | 43 GET routes × 4 samples with Stash query counts, media headers, the Whisparr list, a cache-eviction reproduction |
| Client | production bundle, 15 routes × 2 viewports cold and warm, scroll cost to page 19, hover cost, image variant fit |
| Phone | 18 screens portrait, key ones landscape, every control under 44 px measured, taps-to-goal |
| Desktop | same screens at 1440 and 1920, keyboard help, hover, focus order, a copy table |
| IA | every job-to-be-done as a flow, all 25 mutations traced for freshness, naming, errors |

## Headline findings (numbers from the reports)

**Server** (`server.md`): warm, 41 of 43 routes answer under 10 ms and everything is gzip'd;
the cold paths go cold far too often. One 30-entry `VOLATILE` cap is shared by facets, tag
tiles, `sceneStashIdMap`, every StashDB page and Whisparr's list, so paging the wanted shelf 31
times evicts them all (facets 24 ms → 2.2 s, reproduced; 278 of 401 misses since that day's
restart). The shelf's page 1 downloads Whisparr's whole list, 41 MB / 13,079 entries, every
2 min and after every Get click (3–4 s in the pod log), and hotlinks 24 StashDB covers of
~280 KB per page. Rating, O, edit and performer-favourite call `invalidate('scene')`, which
drops every list (Home rebuilds cold, 0.7–1.9 s); plays use `expire`. Stash change detection
expired everything three times in 14 min during a Whisparr import, re-warming ~20 queries
each time.

**Client** (`client.md`): the bundle is fine (98 KB gzip entry, every page split, hls.js only
for transcodes). Rendering is the cost: a Browse page append re-renders every loaded card
(58 ms at 80 cards → 190 ms at 760), Home renders ~260 cards and re-renders them all every
12 s for the hero timer (a 65–70 ms long task while idle), Tags renders 1,106 tiles with
grid-sized images (LCP 616 ms on the phone). Layout shifts: the station row is inserted
under the hero ~300 ms after paint (CLS 0.10), the wanted shelf above the grid up to 0.9 s
after (0.13–0.17). The video poster is the full 163 KB screenshot; row cards and tiles reuse
the grid's `sizes`; a mouse sweep over three cards downloads 2.6 MB of previews; API
responses carry no ETag so a reload re-downloads every list.

**Phone** (`phone.md`): two outright bugs, seen in screenshots: in landscape the header hits
the desktop breakpoint and the Settings gear is clipped off the right edge
(`Layout.tsx` `overflow-x-clip`), and the Browse "Pin to home" popover paints behind the first
card. Structural: the filter bar wraps to three rows (~290 px) on every list and the wanted
shelf sits above the library, so a performer's own videos start more than a screen down.
Tap targets are systematically under 44 px (header 36, card heart/save 30, stars 22, "+" 24,
pencil 16). The long-press sheet is undiscoverable, lacks Save to playlist and exposes Delete;
Delete sits in the scene page's main action row.

**Desktop** (`desktop.md`): in good shape overall (dense grids, `?` overlay, hover preview and
scrub, right-click menu, focus rings). Every scroll row loses its 24 px left gutter
(`snap-start` with no `scroll-padding`); the wanted shelf precedes the library at both sizes;
white studio logos vanish on the white avatar disc; 40 unnamed links per Browse page and
hover-only buttons that take focus while invisible; three tab-sibling pages with three widths;
"tap" wording on a mouse screen; "Favourite" and "Favorite", "Categories" and "tags",
"1790" and "1,790" on the same page; a bare "Not found" 404.

**IA** (`ia.md`): four concepts share the word "favourite" (video heart, performer/studio
Follow, tag Pin, the "performers I follow" filter), spelt two ways. Mutation failures are
silent: no global error handler, only 4 of 25 write paths show an error, and a failed rating
keeps its optimistic stars. Freshness leaks the user notices: no progress bar on the card you
just watched and a Continue badge that only updates on window focus; Identify's result lost if
you leave the page; a ghost "Everything" station on Home after deleting a playlist; station
cards blank for 10 min after creating one; the Performers-page gender switch silently changes
Home and Stats without refreshing them. Playlist "Shuffle" permanently rewrites the order.
Block has no Undo while Hide has one.

## Wiki corrections made from the evidence

- [[caching]]: Whisparr's list is 41 MB / 13,079 entries (was "~25 MB"); the 30-entry
  `VOLATILE` cap is shared and is the dominant cause of misses. `whisparr.ts` comment too.
- [[whisparr-api]]: same size correction.
- [[scene-page]]: Mark as watched is in the card menu, not on the page.
- [[people-pages]] and [[settings-next-to-page]]: the intro skip is set from the player's
  gear menu, not on the studio page (the decision itself is unchanged).

## Not verified

- No real iPhone: the standalone-mode Back button and the mini-player restore bug
  ([[mini-player]]) stay unverified on the device.
- Nothing that writes was exercised: time to first frame, the HLS start path, the Cache
  "Clear everything" confirmation, the ghost-station bug (from code), the missing-file player
  state (from code).
- The audit ran while someone was using the app and while Whisparr imported, which inflated
  some maxima; the p50s were stable.

## Snags

- Five agents sharing one Vite and one production worked; running them against one Chrome
  would not have. Each script starts its own Chrome in its own outdir.
- `get_page_text` and JavaScript that touches a page token are blocked in the browser tools;
  the Forgejo log reading in the same session used DOM text instead.
